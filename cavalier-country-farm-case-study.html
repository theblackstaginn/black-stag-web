Cavalier Country Farm Website Case Study

Cavalier Country Farm case study: a mobile-first farm website for Cavaliers, family experiences, photography, applications, and trusted breeder tools.

Business type: Family farm & Cavalier King Charles Spaniel breeder · Location: Cleveland, Georgia

View the live site Cleveland, GA web design

The challenge

Cavalier Country Farm needed one website to represent several related parts of farm life without making the visitor choose between a breeder site and a family-farm site.

The site needed to introduce the Cavaliers, farm animals, family experiences, photography opportunities, and application process while keeping the tone calm and personal.

It also needed to work alongside trusted external breeder tools instead of replacing systems that were already useful.

  • Cavalier information and breeder calls to action
  • Farm-animal and family-experience content
  • Photography and seasonal experience information
  • Application and deposit paths
  • Clear mobile navigation

The build

The project uses a focused small-site structure with the homepage supported by dedicated animals and FAQ pages.

The homepage introduces the farm, Cavaliers, experiences, photography, and application path. Supporting pages give visitors somewhere to go when they want more detail without overloading the opening experience.

Application and payment actions connect to the breeder's established Good Dog workflow, preserving a working system while giving the public-facing brand its own website.

  • Custom farm-forward visual design
  • Dedicated animals and FAQ pages
  • Good Dog application and payment integration
  • Facebook and breeder-profile connections
  • Responsive navigation and mobile-first calls to action
  • Custom-domain, owned-code deployment

A hybrid setup on purpose

Ownership does not mean ripping out every third-party tool.

For Cavalier Country Farm, the right architecture was to own the main website while continuing to use Good Dog where it already handled breeder applications and payments well.

That keeps the storefront portable while avoiding an unnecessary rebuild of specialized systems that already work.

The result

The farm now has a central digital home that can represent the full experience instead of sending every visitor directly to a social network or breeder marketplace.

Families can understand the farm, meet the animals, learn about experiences, and then move into the appropriate external application or payment workflow when they are ready.

The site also gives the business room to grow beyond breeding without having to abandon its existing identity.
